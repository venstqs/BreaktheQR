Why are some squares “flipped”?

Before printing, the code is masked: a fixed pattern (one of 8) flips some data squares so there are no big blank areas or fake “eyes” that would confuse a scanner. The format label tells the scanner which mask to undo. Tap a data square to see whether it was flipped.

3 · The zig-zag read order

Data is written in 8-square bundles called codewords. They snake upward and downward in 2-square-wide columns, starting at the bottom-right corner and dodging the eyes and rulers.

Unveiling the technology

How a QR code survives

01

Find it

The three eyes have a 1 : 1 : 3 : 1 : 1 ring pattern that looks the same from any angle. Scanners look for them first, then use the timing rulers and alignment target to map the grid, even when it's tilted.

02

Read the label

The format label is 15 bits saying which error-correction level and mask were used. It's stored twice and has its own mini error correction, so it can lose 3 bits per copy.

03

Collect codewords

The scanner walks the zig-zag path, gathering 8-bit data and backup codewords. Longer codes split them into blocks and shuffle the blocks together, so one scratch spreads its damage thinly.

04

Repair the damage

The backup codewords are Reed–Solomon checksums, the same maths that protects satellite photos, CDs and phone calls. With 2k backup codewords a scanner can find and fix k broken ones, without knowing in advance which ones are broken.

Version explorer

Numbers come from the QR Code specification (ISO/IEC 18004) and our own calculations: grid = (17 + 4v)², eyes = 3 × 7 × 7 = 147 modules, repair limit = backup codewords per block ÷ 2.

Digital safety

Check before you tap

A QR code is just a link in disguise. “Quishing” (QR phishing) scams stick fake codes over real ones on parking meters, posters and emails. They work because we trust the place, scan out of habit, can't see the link, and don't check it.

  1. Pause. Is this code where you'd expect it? Is it a sticker on top of another one?
  2. Read the preview. Your camera shows the link before opening it. Read the domain, the part just before the first single “/”.
  3. Spot the tricks: misspellings (paypa1), extra words (bank-secure-login.xyz), link shorteners, or no “https”.
  4. Never type passwords or payment details on a page you reached from a random code. Go to the official app or website instead.

Evidence

The problem in numbers

QR codes now carry payments, logistics, safety systems and log-ins, yet users see only black and white squares. Research focuses heavily on technical defences, while far fewer studies look at what users understand. That is the gap this project targets.

Limits we state openly

  • The meter is a teaching model. Real scanners can also fail from blur, glare or low contrast, which we mention but don't fully simulate.
  • Damage is counted per codeword. In rare layouts the true decoder may succeed or fail slightly differently, which is why every result is double-checked with jsQR.
  • Eye damage is judged with a simple rule (pupil + ring squares changed); real finder detection is more subtle.
  • No scanned content is uploaded or stored. Camera frames stay on the device.
  • The quishing tip is general safety advice, not a security product.
